# Reverse KL / JSD on student-written text (2026-11-24)

## Question
Run E (half the pool student-written after 1M tokens) tied teacher-led A on held-out KL and halved the KL on the student's own
text, but the teacher's NLL of the student's samples did not improve (E 4.6 vs A 2.2, 8 samples). Hypothesis: forward KL on student
text is mass-covering and does not reward plausible sampling. Does reverse KL or GKD-style JSD on the student-written sequences
fix that without hurting held-out KL, and does it hold at 8.2M tokens?

## Method
- float-train `--ternary`, seeds 0 and 1, 2.05M tokens (default) and 8.19M (`--tokens 8192000 --round 1024000`).
- `--onpolicy-loss fkl|rkl|jsd [--jsd-beta 0.5]` applies only to student-written pool sequences (the pool records the writer);
  teacher-written ones always use forward KL. Exact over the 65536-token vocabulary, gradient through q only:
  rkl = sum q (log q - log p); jsd = b KL(p||m) + (1-b) KL(q||m), m = b p + (1-b) q, log m by logAddExp. The default (fkl) takes the
  old code path unchanged. The logged train KL on student sequences is the training objective (rkl/jsd value), not forward KL.
- E variants: `--onpolicy-const 0.5 --onpolicy-from 1024000` (8.2M: `--onpolicy-from 4096000`), i.e. half of training, then lambda 0.5.
- On-policy eval: 64 samples, fixed key 7777, generated in one batch; mean and standard error (over samples) of forward KL(p||q)
  and teacher NLL per token; alpha; **rep4** = fraction of 4-grams that already occurred earlier in the same sample (repetition
  guard). Reference rep4 of held-out *teacher* text: **0.556**. Teacher NLL on that text is 1.892 nats/token.
- SE is over the 64 samples of one run; it does not include seed-to-seed variation. Raw: `results/runs-2026-11-24/onpolicy2-*`.

## Answer
- **Reverse KL gives lower teacher NLL of the student's samples (A 1.42 -> E-rkl 1.03 at 2.05M; E-fkl is 5.54) and does not hurt
  held-out KL (2.26 vs 2.38, but that is within seed spread, n = 2).** JSD sits between: NLL about the same as A (1.55), the best
  on-policy KL (1.06), and the worst held-out KL (2.43; +0.155 on seed 0).
- **But the NLL metric is confounded by repetition.** Student samples from A, E-rkl and E-jsd have rep4 0.85-0.92, far above the
  teacher's own 0.556: they loop, and looping text has low teacher NLL (the teacher predicts the repeat). So low NLL here mostly
  says "repetitive", not "plausible". E-fkl is the only variant with diverse text (rep4 0.34-0.56, nearest the teacher's) and it has
  by far the worst NLL, so the earlier E-vs-A NLL gap (4.6 vs 2.2) is a diversity effect as much as a quality one. rkl is
  mode-seeking and goes furthest into the repetitive mode (0.92), as expected. I cannot claim rkl/jsd make samples more plausible.
- **8.2M: the picture changes.** All variants repeat less (rep4 0.71-0.75, nearer 0.556) and teacher NLL is indistinguishable
  (A 2.77 vs E-rkl 2.59; per-run SE 0.4). E-rkl halves on-policy KL (0.85 vs 1.60) but loses 0.08 nats of held-out KL (+0.092,
  +0.064: same sign on both seeds, but n = 2). No sample-quality gain is visible at 8.2M by this metric.
- **Uncertainty.** n = 2 seeds; per-run SEs are 0.1-0.4 on NLL and on-policy KL (over samples, not seeds); NLL differences below
  about 0.5 are noise. The rkl held-out advantage at 2.05M comes from seed 1 (-0.256) alone. The variant chosen for 8.2M was
  E-rkl (best held-out KL and NLL at 2.05M). A diversity-aware sample metric (NLL together with rep4 or sample entropy) is needed
  before drawing a conclusion about sample quality.

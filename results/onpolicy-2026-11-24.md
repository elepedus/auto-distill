# On-policy vs teacher-led data, float-train --ternary (2026-11-24)

## Question
The teacher writes the training text today (256 decode steps per sequence, 34 ms/seq). Should the student write some of it,
scored by one parallel teacher pass, and if so when should we switch?

## Method
- Student: float-train `--ternary` (TTF), 2.048M tokens, eval every 256k, default settings, seeds 0 and 1.
- **alpha** = mean over positions of sum_v min(p_teacher, q_student) = 1 - TV (speculative acceptance rate). Computed inside the
  loss from the existing logits (no gradient). Train lines log the mean over the logging window; evals log it on the held-out set.
- **Student text**: `studentGenerate`, cached decode (conv: last 2 inputs; attention: K/V), temperature 1, random first token,
  same (ternary) weights as the trainer. Checked against the full forward: max |logit diff| 3.5e-6 (`--gentest`).
  **8.1 ms/seq at 256 sequences.** The naive full-prefix version was not built; caching was written directly.
- **Teacher scoring**: batched forward in chunks of 32 ([32,256,65536] < 2^31), fp32 logSoftmax, stored bf16; the pool holds
  teacher-decoded and student-written sequences interchangeably, shuffled. Measured cost of a student sequence: about 29 ms
  (8 sampling + about 21 scoring) against 33-34 ms for a teacher-decoded one, so little saving.
- **lambda** = fraction of each 256-sequence pool refill written by the student: `--onpolicy-const L [--onpolicy-from TOKENS]`;
  `--onpolicy-ramp A,B` (0 at A to 1 at B tokens); `--onpolicy-alpha A0,A1` (clamp((abar-A0)/(A1-A0)), abar = mean alpha over
  the last 50 updates). lambda is re-evaluated at each refill (every 65,536 tokens). Default 0 is the old behaviour.
- **Evals** at every eval: held-out KL/alpha on the 8 fixed teacher sequences; on-policy KL (teacher || student), teacher NLL per
  token and alpha on 8 student samples drawn with a FIXED key (global RNG untouched). The teacher's own NLL on the held-out teacher
  text (about its entropy) is **1.892 nats/token**. The on-policy numbers come from only 8 samples and are very noisy: one
  token the teacher finds near-impossible moves teacher NLL by several nats.
- Run A's train-alpha curve (mean over the log window, seed 0): 256k 0.09, 512k 0.27, 768k 0.37, 1.02M 0.48, 1.28M 0.49,
  1.54M 0.47, 1.79M 0.54, 2.05M 0.42. Thresholds chosen for D: A0 = 0.2 (about 0.5M), A1 = 0.5 (about 1.5M).
- Runs: A lambda=0; B `--onpolicy-const 1`; C `--onpolicy-ramp 512000,1536000`; D `--onpolicy-alpha 0.2,0.5`;
  E `--onpolicy-const 0.5 --onpolicy-from 1024000`. Raw: `results/runs-2026-11-24/onpolicy-*.{jsonl,log}`.

## Answer: when to switch
- **On the metric we train (KL on teacher text), do not switch before about 1M tokens, and switching later only ties.**
  Pure student text from the start (B) is clearly worst (3.12 vs 2.19). Switching early (ramp from 0.5M; D, whose alpha trigger
  fired at about 0.5M) costs 0.4-0.5 nats. A half-student pool from 1.02M (train alpha about 0.45-0.5) matches teacher-led:
  +0.09 and -0.03 on two seeds, no measurable difference (seed spread about +-0.1, n = 2).
- **On the student's own text, mixing helps**: on-policy KL is 2-3x lower for E (1.36 vs 2.98 mean); B and D are lower still. That
  is the exposure-bias effect. But the teacher NLL of the student's samples does not improve (E 4.6 vs A 2.2, and the single
  8-sample number swings 1.1-9.6 between evals), so there is no evidence that on-policy training makes student text more
  teacher-plausible. The two rankings disagree, as expected.
- **Speed: not faster here.** A student sequence costs about 29 ms (8 sampling + about 21 for batched scoring, dominated by fp32
  logSoftmax over [32,256,65536]) against 33-34 ms for teacher decoding. End to end the runs are 454-552 s against 502 s
  (C excluded). Scoring would need a cheaper path (bf16 logSoftmax, or top-k targets) to pay off.
- **Alpha as a trigger**: D's alpha 0.2-0.5 fired too early, because training alpha on teacher text is noisy (window values
  range 0.09-0.64 around 0.5M) and rises faster once the pool contains student text. If switching at all, wait for abar >= 0.45
  (about 1M tokens) and keep lambda <= 0.5. Uncertainty: 2 seeds, one 2M-token budget, 8-sample on-policy evals; treat
  differences under about 0.15 nats as noise.

# Lean TTF: how little optimiser state does ternary-native training need?

Question: TTF keeps a fp32 accumulator A per weight (4 B/param) on top of the trits. How much of it can go?
Testbed: `float-train --ternary` (tiny LFM2-shaped student distilled from LFM2-350M), default hyper. Metric: final held-out KL (nats).

## Variants (`--acc`, kernel `ttfLeanKernel` in Sources/LFM2ANE/Shared.swift; `ttfKernel` untouched)
| acc | accumulator state | measured B/param (acc buffer, size x itemsize) |
|---|---|---|
| fp32 | float32 (old kernel, bit-identical) | 4 |
| fp16 | half | 2 |
| int8 | int8 in units of theta/127; stochastic rounding on write-back, r from a counter hash of (index, step); saturates at +-theta | 1 |
| stateless | none; trit steps toward sign(u) with prob min(1, pscale*|u|/theta) | 0 |

Factored row/col moments are unchanged (negligible). Caveat: in float-train the trits themselves sit in fp16 buffers (2 B/param) and the
kernel's per-step `moved`/`acc_out` outputs are transient; the 2-bit packed trit store is a separate issue. The "B/param" above is optimiser state only.
Stateless variance note: drift matches the non-leaky accumulator, but each step is a Bernoulli draw, so net-movement variance is ~|u|*theta per step
versus a bounded residual (<theta) for the accumulator, and there is no leak/averaging of gradient noise.

## 2.05M tokens, final held-out KL
| acc | seed 0 | seed 1 |
|---|---|---|
| fp32 | 2.218 | 2.443 |
| fp16 | 2.189 | 2.435 |
| int8 | 2.169 | 2.467 |
| stateless | 5.456 | 5.682 |
| stateless, pscale 0.1 (tweak, seed 0) | 4.927 | - |

Curves (seed 0, held-out KL at 0 / 0.51M / 1.02M / 1.54M / 2.05M tokens):
- fp32: 9.17 3.80 2.72 2.35 2.22
- fp16: 9.17 3.61 2.58 2.31 2.19
- int8: 9.17 3.81 2.78 2.34 2.17
- stateless: 9.17 5.59 5.36 5.92 5.46
- stateless pscale 0.1: 9.17 6.00 5.36 5.00 4.93

Stateless lags fp32 by >3 nats. Diagnosis: it flips ~15x more trits per step than fp32 (jsonl `flips`, e.g. 48k vs 3.4k at update 500, 10.6k vs 57 at update 7000): without the accumulator
nothing averages out gradient noise, and the flip rate does not anneal. Tweak: probability x0.1 (via rho): helped (5.46 -> 4.93) but nowhere near closing the gap. Not tried: lower pscale, or pscale annealed with the lr schedule.

## 8.2M tokens, seed 0 (`--tokens 8192000 --round 1024000`)
| acc | final KL | curve (0 / 2M / 4M / 6M / 8.2M) |
|---|---|---|
| fp32 | 1.857 | 9.17 2.46 2.18 1.96 1.86 |
| int8 | 1.847 | 9.17 2.58 2.26 2.01 1.85 |

Earlier references (pre fused-target): 1.832 / 1.811. The re-measured fp32 is 1.857.

## Conclusion
- The fp32 accumulator is not needed: int8 (1 B/param) matches fp32 within seed noise at 2.05M (diffs -0.05, +0.02; seed-to-seed spread ~0.23) and at 8.2M (1.847 vs 1.857). fp16 likewise. So optimiser state drops 4 -> 1 B/param at no measurable cost.
- Zero state (stateless) does not work in this form: >3 nats worse, and the 0.1x tweak only recovers ~0.5. The accumulator's job is noise filtering/annealing the flip rate, which a memoryless rule cannot do. 1 byte, not 0, is the demonstrated floor; sub-byte (4-bit A) is untested.
- Uncertainty: 2 seeds at 2.05M, 1 at 8.2M, one tiny model; differences below ~0.1 nats are not resolvable. Only int8 was run at 8.2M (fp16 was not).
